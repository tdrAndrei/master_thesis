\section{\label{sec:hou}Higher-order unification}

This section summarises the higher-order unification (HOU) problems that arise
when elaborating a dependently typed language, and Miller's \emph{pattern
fragment}, the decidable subset of HOU that our implementation targets.

\subsection{Why type checking requires HOU}
Inference in dependently typed languages is triggered by underscores
\texttt{\_}, written by the user or, more often, inserted by the elaborator for
implicit arguments (\cref{sec:statix}). Every underscore is replaced by a fresh
\emph{metavariable} \texttt{?M}, and whenever a typing rule requires two open
terms to be equal, those metavariables become unknowns in a \emph{unification
problem}. Often this problem is first-order. For example, in Agda
\autocite{norell2009dependently}, checking
$\mathrm{refl}\;\mathbb{N}\;42 : f\;\_ \equiv f\;42$ yields the constraint
$f\;?M \doteq f\;42$, which decomposes on the rigid head $f$ into
$?M \doteq 42$.

Because types may depend on values, however, a metavariable can occur under
binders, where its solution must mention bound variables. Consider a vector
lookup function whose return type is left to inference:
\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Higher-order constraint arising from an underscore under a binder}]
lookup : (A: Set) -> (n: Nat) -> (xs: Vec A n) -> (i: Fin n) -> _
lookup A n xs i = ...
\end{lstlisting}
The underscore is in the scope of \texttt{A}, \texttt{n},
\texttt{xs}, and \texttt{i}, so its metavariable $?M$ may depend on them. When
the body is found to return a value of type $A$, this produces the constraint
$?M\;[A\;n\;\mathit{xs}\;i] \doteq A$, which no closed term solves. The unknown
stands for a \emph{function}, and the problem is higher-order.

\subsection{Metavariable solutions are functions}
Metavariables that depend on their context can be represented as closures with
a suspended substitution \autocite{nanevski2008contextual}, or, following
Miller \autocite{miller_logic_1991}, as functions that are applied to the
distinct bound variables in scope when they are created. We use the latter:
$?M\;[A\;n\;\mathit{xs}\;i]$ becomes $?M\;A\;n\;\mathit{xs}\;i$, at the cost of
an extra beta reduction once $?M$ is solved by a lambda term.

Writing every term in spine form $h\;\vec{e}$, a head $h$ is \emph{rigid} if it
is a constant or bound variable and \emph{flex} if it is a metavariable. A
unification problem $\Gamma \vdash t_1 \doteq t_2$ is then classified by its two
heads \autocite{miller_logic_1991}. \emph{Rigid-rigid} problems are solvable if and only if
the heads coincide and the argument spines unify pairwise. \emph{Flex-rigid} problems $?M\;\vec{e} \doteq t$ require inverting
the application of $?M$, which is non-trivial in general because $\vec{e}$ may
contain arbitrary terms, repeated variables, or other metavariables.
\emph{Flex-flex} problems $?M\;\vec{e} \doteq ?N\;\vec{e'}$ generally have
infinitely many solutions and cannot be committed to without further
information.

\subsection{The pattern fragment}
\label{section:patternfrag}
General HOU is undecidable, and solvable problems need not have a most general
unifier. A problem $\Gamma \vdash ?M\;\vec{e} \doteq t$ is in the \emph{pattern
fragment} when $\vec{e}$ consists of pairwise distinct bound variables (up to
$\eta$-expansion), and the same holds for every metavariable application in
$t$. Miller showed that within this fragment unification is decidable and
yields most general unifiers \autocite{miller_logic_1991}. The solving rule is
direct:
\begin{mathpar}
  \inferrule*[Right=pat-flex-rigid]
    {x_1,\dots,x_n \text{ distinct bound vars} \\\\ \mathrm{FV}(t) \subseteq \{x_1,\dots,x_n\} \\ ?M \notin t}
    {\Gamma \vdash ?M\;x_1\cdots x_n \;\doteq\; t \;\;\rightsquigarrow\;\; ?M := \lambda x_1\cdots x_n.\,t}
\end{mathpar}
Distinct variables make the substitution trivially invertible, the
free-variable (\emph{scope}) check keeps the solution within $?M$'s context, and
the occurs check prevents cyclic solutions. Flex-flex problems within the
fragment are solved by a fresh metavariable over the intersection of the two
variable lists \autocite{abel_extensions_2018}.

\subsubsection{Outside the pattern fragment}\label{section:outside_pattern_fragment}
A problem falls outside the fragment when a metavariable is applied to a
non-variable term ($?M\;0$), a repeated variable ($?M\;x\;x$), or an unsolved
metavariable. An elaborator can then either fail eagerly, which is
simple and predictable but rejects programs whose constraints would later have
become solvable, or postpone the constraint until other constraints
instantiate the offending metavariables. For example, $?M\;x\;x \doteq x$ has
the two solutions $\lambda x\,y.\,x$ and $\lambda x\,y.\,y$. Eager failure
rejects it, whereas after postponement a later constraint may fix
$?M := \lambda x\,y.\,y$ and reduce the problem to $x \doteq x$. This
\emph{dynamic pattern unification} \autocite{abel_extensions_2018} is used by
the elaborators of Agda and Lean. It is complemented by \emph{pruning}, which
removes a metavariable's dependency on a variable that would otherwise escape
the scope check of \textsc{pat-flex-rigid} \autocite{abel_extensions_2018}.
Neither is integrated in our implementation yet (\cref{sec:future-work}).
